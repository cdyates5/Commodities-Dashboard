Methodology & data

An independent, demand-only lead index for commodity prices, in the spirit of the growth/liquidity demand leg of a Variant Perception-style capital-cycle model — built here as a standalone signal.

The index. A weighted blend of two standardized engines: (1) a commodity-demand-weighted global growth LEI — the six-country growth leading indicators (China, US, Euro Area, Japan, UK, Australia), weighted toward commodity-intensive economies; and (2) the China credit impulse — the one-year change in China's credit-to-GDP ratio, the classic policy-driven lead for industrial demand. Default weights are 60% growth / 40% credit impulse, chosen to maximize forward predictive power; the slider lets you re-mix them.

How it leads. Global growth momentum is largely coincident with commodity prices — both are cyclical at the same time — so on its own it leads by only a couple of months. The China credit impulse supplies the genuine forward lead (peaks ~9–18 months out). Blended, the index leads broad commodity prices by roughly 5 months and keeps positive forward-return correlation out to 12–18 months (IC ≈ +0.2 to +0.28). Toggle to metals and the picture sharpens: growth momentum there mean-reverts at longer horizons, so the credit impulse carries the forward signal — a reason to lean on the credit weight for metals.

Sources & honest limitations

Sources. Six national growth LEIs (reused from the Global Growth Leading Indicators build; each a composite of yield curve, credit spreads, permits, claims, sentiment, hours, capex and money); China credit-to-GDP from the BIS; OECD composite leading indicators and Korea export growth as cross-checks; commodity price indices (IMF all-commodities, metals, energy, industrial inputs), year-on-year.

Limitations. (1) This is a demand gauge only — it says nothing about supply, inventories or the term structure, and should be read alongside the supply and carry models. (2) Growth momentum is coincident-to-leading; the honest lead comes chiefly from the credit impulse, which is quarterly (BIS) and interpolated, with the latest quarter carried forward. (3) Weights are fixed and standardization is full-sample. (4) Country LEI weights are approximate commodity-demand shares, not a formal optimization. None of this is investment advice.
