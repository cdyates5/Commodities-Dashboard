Methodology & data

This is an independent reconstruction of Variant Perception's commodity supply-demand framework. The logic mirrors theirs; the data is rebuilt from freely available sources.

Supply — capital cycle. VP defines capital-scarcity from three fundamental ratios: Capex+R&D / Assets (inverted), D&A / Assets, and ROIC. Under-investment relative to the asset base today constrains future supply and is bullish for prices ~12 months out. Here the model's supply line is built exactly this way from the US mining & energy sector's own accounts: capex/assets and D&A/assets reconstructed from the Census Quarterly Financial Report, plus sector return on assets, each standardized and averaged into one capital-scarcity signal. The live company table below applies the same three ratios cross-sectionally to today's listed producers. Sector fundamentals on this grid begin in 2003, so the combined model starts then.

Demand — growth & liquidity. A composite of leading, demand-relevant market signals: EM-equity momentum, high-yield credit momentum, dollar weakness and the rate impulse — standing in for VP's "median growth LEI / liquidity" demand proxy.

Model. Each driver is standardized; the model is their weighted blend, read forward by the lead horizon. Peak in-sample lead is ~11-14 months (correlation ≈ +0.44), consistent with VP's "capex leads commodity prices by 12 months."

Data sources & honest limitations

Sources. S&P GSCI; Census Quarterly Financial Report (mining & energy capex, depreciation, assets, income) for the supply line; the dollar index, 10-year yields, EM equities and high-yield credit for demand — monthly; live company fundamentals (capex, D&A, total assets, EBIT, invested capital) for ~46 commodity producers.

Limitations. (1) The supply line is the sector's genuine Capex/Assets, D&A/Assets and ROA from the Census QFR (extractive core: oil, gas, mining), so it now agrees with the live company table — both read capital-scarce today. Capex is reconstructed from the balance sheet (ΔNet PP&E + depreciation); the latest quarter (Q1 2026) is carried forward for the most recent months, and the common history with demand begins in 2003. (2) Demand uses market-implied signals that are partly coincident; the genuine lead comes mainly from the liquidity tilt and the capital cycle. (3) Standardization is full-sample. (4) Fundamentals cover recent fiscal years only, so the table is a current snapshot, not a backtest. None of this is investment advice.
