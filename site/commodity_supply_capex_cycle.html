Methodology & data

An independent reconstruction of Variant Perception's commodity capex-cycle chart. The economic logic is theirs; the series are rebuilt from public data.

The signal. Capex / Total Assets for the US mining & energy sector, built from the Census Bureau's Quarterly Financial Report: capex is reconstructed as the change in net property, plant & equipment plus depreciation charged; total assets are reported directly. The ratio (~3-16% across the cycle) is inverted and read forward, exactly as VP plot it. Under-investment relative to the asset base constrains future supply and is bullish for prices; over-investment does the reverse.

Prices. A broad commodity price index, 2-year percent change — the same momentum framing as VP's "Bloomberg Commodity Total Return 2y % Chg." Toggle between the all-commodities and energy indices; energy aligns a touch better since the QFR mining sector is oil & gas-heavy.

Lead. On this data the signal leads by roughly 18-21 months at peak correlation (r ≈ +0.4, a little shorter on the energy index), somewhat longer than VP's cited 12 — the QFR-derived capex flow is noisier than cash capex and carries some impairment effect. The chart defaults to a 12-month lead to match their framing; the empirical peak is shown in the cards and the lead-lag chart, and the lead is adjustable.

Sources & honest limitations

Sources. Census Quarterly Financial Report, All Mining (total assets, net PP&E, accumulated depreciation, depreciation, income); IMF all-commodities and energy price indices; live company fundamentals (capex, D&A, total assets, EBIT, invested capital) for ~36 listed producers.

Limitations. (1) QFR "All Mining" covers oil, gas and mining corporations — the extractive core of the commodity complex, not agriculture or downstream. (2) Capex is reconstructed from the balance sheet rather than taken from cash-flow statements, so it is smoother-but-noisier than VP's Capex+R&D and can be distorted by asset write-downs (e.g. 2016). (3) The composite uses ROA in place of ROIC at the sector level; the company table uses true ROIC. (4) Standardization is full-sample; the price index is spot, used as a total-return proxy.
